Breaking news

Once you have passed a few piles, headlines start to hit. A coloured news bar flashes across the top for a couple of seconds, then shrinks to a small label with a draining timer. While it runs, the rules change.

  • Inflation Piles swell

    New piles are one and a half times wider, and the gaps between them shrink by a fifth.

  • China tariffs Red piles, fast

    New piles come in red with gold straps, and everything scrolls a third faster.

  • War Incoming missiles

    The sky darkens and missiles fly in at nearly twice the scroll speed. Touch one and you are done.

  • Rate cut Everything floats

    Gravity drops by almost half, flaps get softer and new gaps open up. A breather.

  • Election Double points

    Every pile is worth two points, and the world scrolls a little faster to make you earn them.

  • Immunity Untouchable

    The one good headline. For eight seconds the head flashes every colour and bulldozes straight through piles and missiles, a little faster than usual. Only the ground can still end it.

  • Shutdown Lights out

    Everything goes dark except a spotlight around the head. The piles are still there; you just cannot see them coming.

  • Stimulus Free money

    Loose bills drift in between the piles. Every one you touch is a point, on top of the usual.

  • Hurricane Gusts

    The sky turns grey, the rain comes in sideways and gusts shove the head up and down while you try to line up.

When they happen
Nothing before a score of three. After that, every pile you pass rolls the dice, unless an event is already running or the short cooldown after the last one is still ticking. The same headline never hits twice in a row.
What they never do
A pile you are already lined up for never changes shape or moves. Only piles spawned during the event are affected, so a headline is never an instant, unfair death.

About

Where it came from

Trappy Flump started as a Flappy Bird clone that ran inside a terminal, drawing real pixel graphics with coloured half-block characters instead of ASCII art. The bird became a certain blond-swooped head, the pipes became piles of cash, and the breaking-news events followed.

The Android app is the same game rebuilt for a phone: same sprites, same physics, same headlines, tuned for a portrait screen and a tapping thumb. The version on this page is that game, running in your browser.

What it is not

This is satire. The characters are caricatures drawn at twelve pixels wide, and the game is not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to any real person, party, government or news organisation.

Nothing here is a comment on anyone's actual policies. It is a comment on how it feels to read the news while trying to keep a small head in the air.
